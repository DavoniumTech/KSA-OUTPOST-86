# Science Notes and Training-Model Boundaries

KSA OUTPOST 86 uses established NASA educational information to explain why an outpost needs connected life-support, power, water, food, thermal and crew-protection systems. Its meters, resource units, event timing and game outcomes are invented classroom values. They are not engineering specifications, measurements, limits or flight rules.

## Environmental Control and Life Support Systems (ECLSS)

NASA describes ECLSS as a group of systems that control or provide functions including cabin pressure, oxygen levels, ventilation, waste management and water supply. NASA identifies the Water Recovery System, Air Revitalization System and Oxygen Generation System as distinct components.

The game therefore does not use a single “air” number as a substitute for all life support. It displays separate simplified indicators for:

- Oxygen supply.
- Cabin-air condition.
- Carbon-dioxide-control condition.
- Water reserve and water-recovery condition.

The game’s water-recovery malfunction is deliberately not coded as an automatic oxygen-generation failure. The relationship between water and oxygen generation is described in the NASA ECLSS reference, but the simulated fault is scoped to the water recovery unit so learners can isolate a system dependency.

Source: NASA, [Environmental Control and Life Support Systems (ECLSS)](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/environmental-control-and-life-support-systems-eclss/).

## Water recovery

NASA describes a process that reclaims wastewater and humidity condensate, treats it through filtration and catalytic processing, checks water quality and returns acceptable water to storage. The game simplifies this to a reserve meter, recovery-condition meter, repair choice and conservation choice.

The game's recovery rate and resource changes are not copied from the International Space Station or asserted as a lunar habitat performance guarantee.

Source: NASA, [Environmental Control and Life Support Systems (ECLSS)](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/environmental-control-and-life-support-systems-eclss/).

## Lunar south-pole environment

NASA explains that the Sun stays close to the horizon near the lunar poles. Local terrain can create long shadows, and some crater floors may remain permanently shadowed. These conditions affect illumination, solar power planning and thermal design.

The Day 2 low-sunlight event is a fictional, deterministic teaching scenario inspired by those real polar lighting challenges. It does not represent a specific mapped site, ephemeris prediction or real-time solar forecast.

Sources:

- NASA, [The Lunar South Pole Region](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/moonbase-environment/).
- NASA Scientific Visualization Studio, [Shadows near the Moon’s South Pole](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/shadows-near-the-moons-south-pole/).

## Radiation procedure

The Day 4 event teaches a simple operational idea: pause outside work and follow the designated crew-protection procedure when Mission Control issues a warning. The shelter is represented as a protected area, not as a place where radiation disappears.

The game does not calculate absorbed dose, radiation quality factor, shielding effectiveness, storm timing, safe exposure duration or human exposure limits. The alert is not a forecast or safety procedure for real missions.

## Greenhouse and food

The greenhouse advances gradually and uses game water and power. It supplements stored food and does not instantly generate unlimited provisions. The crop-growth meter is a teaching device, not a validated crop model. No plant species, yield, lighting schedule, nutrient recipe or lunar agriculture performance claim is represented.

## Thermal control and crew readiness

Thermal condition is a simplified equipment/habitat training indicator. Crew readiness is a general operational game score influenced by rest and elapsed mission days. It is not a medical or physiological assessment and must not be interpreted as a clinical measurement.

## Training values

All percentage meters and energy units should be understood as:

**KSA TRAINING SIMULATION VALUES — NOT NASA ENGINEERING SPECIFICATIONS.**

The game is intended for introductory systems thinking and discussion, not engineering design, astronaut training certification, mission planning or operational decisions.
